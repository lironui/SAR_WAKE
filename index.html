Seeing 100-km wakes

7,122 Sentinel-1A/B SAR scenes reveal that near-surface wake signatures at 10 m height from large offshore wind farm clusters can persist for more than 100 km under favorable conditions.

01 / KEY FINDINGS

What do the observations tell us?

A multi-regional, multi-year census turns isolated cases into quantifiable observational evidence.

01

LONG-RANGE SIGNATURE

The spatial reach of wakes may exceed conventional planning assumptions

North Sea cases show that very stable boundary layers suppress turbulent mixing and slow wind-field recovery. When several large farms align with the prevailing wind, superposed wakes can extend the near-surface disturbance even farther.

0100+ km
02

MEAN DEFICIT

0.990 m/s

Mean near-surface wind-speed deficit

Equivalent to 12.4% of the upstream wind speed, based on 3,789 valid cases with slower downstream winds.

03

OBSERVED CASES

64.0%

Valid samples showing downstream slowdown

Of 5,920 quantitative samples, 3,789 had lower downstream speeds; about 10% showed deficits above 30%.

04

CROSS-BOUNDARY INTERACTION

Wakes do not stop at borders

SAR imagery captures near-surface wake interactions crossing exclusive economic zones. In densely developed basins such as the North Sea, planning must account for upstream projects in neighboring countries and coordinate potential wake losses across borders.

“Large wind-farm wakes increasingly resemble a regional flow phenomenon, not merely a local engineering effect.”

02 / METHOD

From radar backscatter to wake regions

The paper introduces Restricted-Region Gradient-Guided Growing (R²G³) to extract upstream reference and downstream wake regions in a consistent workflow.

VALIDATION

945 Buoy–SAR matched observations

Six NDBC buoy stations validate the SAR wind retrieval. Correlations above 0.9 at every station support the workflow for analyzing relative wind-speed changes over large offshore areas.

05 / INTERPRETATION

How to interpret the results

10 m

Near-surface flow is not hub-height flow

SAR results represent neutral wind speed at 10 m and cannot be extrapolated directly to the 50–150 m rotor layer without modeling or in-situ measurements.

ΔU

A statistical result is not a project-specific loss

0.990 m/s is a climatological mean across thousands of independent observations, not a fixed loss for any farm pair or weather condition.

R²G³

Real-world boundaries remain uncertain

The algorithm still uses manually selected parameters; image quality, crosswinds, operating state, and low-speed retrieval error can all affect detection.
